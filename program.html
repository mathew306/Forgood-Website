One program, three parts

Strength, nutrition and habits aren't separate services here — they're one coaching relationship, built around the same weekly rhythm: check in, adjust, repeat.

Squat · Hinge · Push · Pull · Carry

Strength

Five patterns, built at your pace

Every program — whatever your starting point — is built on the same five movement patterns: squatting, hinging, pushing, pulling and carrying load. They cover almost everything your body needs to do in daily life, from getting off the floor to moving furniture.

We start with the version of each pattern your joints and experience can handle today, then add load, range of motion or complexity as you're ready for it. Nothing is rushed to hit a number on a chart.

If something doesn't feel right in your body, the plan changes that session — not three weeks later.

What a training week looks like

Most clients train two or three times a week. Each session follows the same shape, so you always know what's coming.

Warm-up

Wake the joints up

Ten minutes of mobility and activation work targeted at whatever you're lifting that day — not a generic stretch routine.

Main lift

Build the pattern

One or two primary lifts where we add load or complexity, coached closely so your technique holds under the new weight.

Accessory & carry

Round it out

Supporting work for weak links, plus a carry or conditioning finisher that mirrors real-life effort — not just more reps.

Nutrition

Fewer rules, better defaults

Most nutrition plans fail because they ask you to be a different person than you actually are. We start from your current routine — what you already eat, when, and with whom — and adjust it, rather than replacing it wholesale.

The one rule we hold firm on is protein: enough at each meal to support the strength you're building and keep you full. Everything else — carbs, fats, timing — flexes around your life and your goals.

No foods are off-limits. We talk about how something fits into your week, not whether you're "allowed" to have it.

Protein first, everything else flexes

What nutrition coaching includes

This isn't a separate service bolted onto training — it's part of every check-in.

Baseline

Start with what you eat now

We review a normal week for you before changing anything, so adjustments build on habits you already have.

Weekly check-in

Small, steady adjustments

One or two changes at a time — a protein target, a swap, a new go-to meal — reviewed and refined each week.

Real life

Built for travel and takeout

Restaurant strategies, travel defaults and "bad week" plans, so one busy stretch doesn't undo your progress.

Small routines, repeated on purpose

Habits

Training is only one input

Two sessions a week can't outrun five nights of bad sleep or a stress load that never lets up. So habits coaching isn't an add-on — it's how we protect the return on the training you're already doing.

We track a small number of daily basics — sleep, steps, stress, consistency — and treat them with the same structure as a lift: start where you are, add one layer at a time, and don't chase perfect.

The goal isn't a rigid routine. It's a handful of habits sturdy enough to survive a bad week.

How we build habits that last

Same weekly rhythm as training: check in, adjust, repeat.

Pick one

One habit at a time

We choose the single habit that will move the needle most right now — usually sleep — and leave everything else alone.

Check in

Weekly, not daily, pressure

A short weekly review keeps you honest without turning every day into a scorecard.

Layer up

Add the next one

Once a habit holds for a few weeks on its own, we layer in the next — recovery, stress, consistency.
